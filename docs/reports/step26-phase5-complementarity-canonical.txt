19. EVIDENCE-BACKED STEP-2.7 ASSESSMENT
====================================================================================================
  The question this phase exists to answer: does TIGER contribute target hits that are
  meaningfully complementary to SASRec and Two-Tower?  At K=10, on the frozen 20 000-user
  cohort, under one shared evaluator:

    TIGER-only hits outside SASRec u Two-Tower   138 users   (0.0069 of the cohort)
    oracle union coverage  |S u T| 460  ->  |S u T u G| 598   (+138, +30.0% relative)
    P(TIGER hit | BOTH others miss) = 138 / 19540 = 0.007062
    standalone hits: SASRec 270, Two-Tower 287, TIGER 277

  The two conclusions the instruction asks to keep apart:

  * STANDALONE recommendation quality: TIGER is comparable to, not better than, the existing
    learned retrievers (277 vs SASRec 270 and Two-Tower 287 at K=10).  On its own this would
    NOT justify a new candidate source -- which is exactly why the decision must not be made
    from standalone Recall.
  * INCREMENTAL candidate-source value: strong.  TIGER reaches 138 users that BOTH existing
    learned retrievers miss: 30.0% of their union and 0.69% of the cohort.  List-level
    overlap is modest (Top-10 Jaccard 0.2460 vs SASRec, 0.1899 vs Two-Tower), and the unique-hit
    contribution is stable across K (99 at K=5, 138 at K=10, 207 at K=20), so it is not a
    head-only or tail-only artefact.

  Classification, descriptive and carrying its counts: SUBSTANTIAL UNIQUE-HIT CONTRIBUTION
  (138 unique users outside SASRec u Two-Tower; +30.0% relative oracle union gain at K=10).

  Step 2.7 CandidateSource evaluation IS therefore justified on this evidence.  That is a
  statement about incremental retrieval value, not about the model being strong in absolute
  terms, and it does not itself authorise any integration: AGENTS.md section 19.2 rule 8 still
  requires a separate, pre-registered step before TIGER becomes an Agent candidate source.
