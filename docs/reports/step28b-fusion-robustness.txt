13. EVIDENCE-BACKED ROBUSTNESS CONCLUSION
====================================================================================================
  Three quantities, kept separate as the contract requires:

  (a) POINT ESTIMATE and DIRECTIONAL ROBUSTNESS -- POSITIVE and CONSISTENT.
      D beats A on all nine quantities under the accepted asymmetric policy, and D20 beats A20
      on all nine under the common-depth control.  Two independent depth policies, same
      direction, every quantity: this is not a single-configuration artefact.

  (b) UNCERTAINTY -- WIDE, and under the accepted policy not distinguishable from zero.
      exact McNemar p = 0.4154 at K=10; paired NDCG bootstrap CI [-0.000155, +0.001125]
      includes zero at all K.  With 20 000 users the discordant pairs still split ~50/50,
      which is the quantitative meaning of 'the +13 net is within cohort noise'.

  (c) DEPTH ROBUSTNESS -- direction robust, MECHANISM not.
      Direction survives depth symmetry, but the mechanism changes: TIGER's captured unique
      coverage FALLS from 50.0%% to 36.2%% at K=10 while the net gain RISES from +13 to +22.
      So the depth-20 improvement is not more TIGER complementarity; it is RRF re-weighting
      three sources whose heads are now equally shallow.  The two must not be conflated.

  Honest summary: rank-only RRF with equal weights produces a small, directionally robust
  improvement when TIGER joins the accepted two-source fusion, but on the accepted policy
  that improvement is NOT statistically distinguishable from cohort noise, and the largest
  part of the oracle complementarity (138 unique users) is only about half captured.

14. IS WEIGHTED-RRF EXPERIMENTATION JUSTIFIED?
====================================================================================================
  Justified as a NEW, separately pre-registered milestone -- and the evidence argues for it
  for a different reason than Step 2.8 did.

  Supportive:
    * the direction is robust across two depth policies and nine quantities;
    * under depth symmetry the paired NDCG CI excludes zero at every K and exact McNemar
      reaches p = 0.0297 at K=20, so a reliably-detectable effect is plausibly reachable;
    * the asymmetric uncertainty is wide precisely because a 30x-deeper-head source and a
      shallow-head source are being given equal weight, which is the question weights address.
  Constraining:
    * on the ACCEPTED policy the effect is not established at all, so weights tuned on the test
      cohort would be fitting noise;
    * no uncertainty analysis can license choosing weights on test.

  Therefore the recommended next step is NOT 'tune weights on test'.  It is, in order:
    1. measure the accepted-policy fusion on the VALIDATION cohort, where the current result is
       the only thing that matters: is the direction there too, and is it any larger;
    2. if and only if it is, select and FREEZE weights on validation targets, hash the frozen
       configuration, and evaluate ONCE on test;
    3. never use the test cohort for weight search, and never report a test-selected weight as
       a result.
  No weighted, calibrated or learned fusion was run in this milestone.
