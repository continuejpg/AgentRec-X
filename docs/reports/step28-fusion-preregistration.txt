--- 1. fusion rule (reused, not chosen here) ---
  implementation   recommendation.control.candidate_ledger.reciprocal_rank_fusion
  constant         RRF_K = 60, the repository default, REUSED unchanged
  form             score(d) = sum over sources of 1 / (60 + rank_source(d))
  rank-only        no raw model score enters the fused value; no normalisation, no weights
  why              the sources score on incomparable scales; rank is the one cross-source
                   quantity the ledger preserves verbatim for every source
  The constant is NOT selected by inspecting TIGER fusion performance.  It is the accepted
  value that already produced the historical controls, and the historical control is
  reproduced bit-exactly under it as a precondition.

--- 2. source weights ---
  all source weights = 1.  No weighting, no grid search, no fitting against the target.

--- 3. candidate depth per source (recorded explicitly) ---
  SASRec      1000   the historical fusion contract (HEAD=1000, Phase-5 value)
  Two-Tower   1000   the same
  TIGER       full certified frontier: min 20 / mean 33.919 / max 667 (measured, per user)
  THE ASYMMETRY IS ACKNOWLEDGED AND RUNS AGAINST TIGER: TIGER supplies a much SHALLOWER head
  than the other two, so it can contribute RRF mass to fewer ranks.  TIGER was NOT given a
  deeper window, and no depth was tuned.
  SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS, DECLARED NOW: a second pass caps EVERY source at a common depth of 20
  (TIGER's minimum), so all three contribute over identical ranks.  Both are reported; the
  primary is the historical-depth contract and the common-depth pass is a sensitivity check,
  never a post-hoc selection.

--- 4. identity, dedup and tie-breaking ---
  flow: source ranking -> item id -> canonical identity -> existing dedup -> RRF -> evaluator
  dedup: experiments.benchmark_public.fused_head, order-preserving first-occurrence dedup
  tail: unmatched catalogue items appended in ascending item id (documented convention)
  tie-break: the shared evaluator's frozen contract; fusion only supplies an order
  Semantic IDs are never fused; only resolved item identities are.
